\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Self-Stabilization in Distributed Systems}

Self-stabilization, introduced by Dijkstra~\cite{dijkstra_self_stab} and formalized
by Dolev~\cite{dolev_self_stab}, enables recovery from any transient fault within bounded
steps without external intervention.
The closure-and-convergence framework used here is due to Arora and
Gouda~\cite{arora_gouda}; Awerbuch and Varghese~\cite{awerbuch_varghese} established the
program-checking paradigm for self-stabilizing protocols.
\emph{Practical self-stabilization}---recovery under parametric assumptions---was
studied by Dolev, Kat, and Meirovitch~\cite{dolev_kat_practical}; our protocol satisfies
this definition with the phase-transition condition ($\sigma^2\beta^2 < 0.25$) as the
assumption boundary.
Recent SSS work extends stabilization to learning-augmented settings~\cite{sss_ml_routing},
federated security~\cite{sss_fl_leakage}, and Byzantine-resilient
blockchain sharding~\cite{sss_blockchain_sharding}.

\subsection{Byzantine Fault Tolerance}

Byzantine agreement~\cite{byzantine_generals} and PBFT~\cite{bft_consensus} are the
classical baselines.
Abraham, Amit, and Dolev~\cite{abraham_byz_stab} provide self-stabilizing Byzantine
consensus, directly relevant to our blockchain substrate.
Garay, Kiayias, and Leonardos~\cite{garay_backbone} formally establish the persistence
and liveness properties of blockchain-based BFT consensus that we rely on.
Recent SSS work examines Byzantine reliable broadcast~\cite{sss_byz_broadcast,sss_byz_counter}.

\subsection{Byzantine-Robust Federated Learning}

Krum~\cite{krum} and coordinate-wise robust statistics~\cite{byzantine_ml} achieve the
minimax-optimal rate $O(\sigma\beta/\sqrt{T})$; our protocol matches this rate via spectral
filtering with the additional self-stabilization guarantee.
Bulyan~\cite{bulyan}, FLTrust~\cite{crfl}, SignGuard~\cite{signguard}, FLAME~\cite{flame},
and ByzShield~\cite{byzshield} address specific attack classes without self-stabilization.
Pillutla, Kakade, and Harchaoui~\cite{geometric_median} achieve robust aggregation via
geometric median; Karimireddy, He, and Jaggi~\cite{karimireddy_bucketing} handle Non-IID
data via history-based bucketing; El~Mhamdi et al.~\cite{elmhamdi_jungle} provide
information-theoretic lower bounds under Non-IID and asynchrony;
Farhadkhani et al.~\cite{farhadkhani_byzsgd} unify several defenses under gradient
decomposition.
\emph{None} of the above provides a formal recovery time bound from an arbitrarily
corrupted initial state under the Arora-Gouda closure-and-convergence framework~\cite{arora_gouda}.

\subsection{Spectral Methods, RMT, and Blockchain FL}

Spectral methods appear in data poisoning defenses~\cite{steinhardt_certified} and neural
network covariance analysis~\cite{rmt_gradients,rmt_neural,spectral_analysis}.
Our contribution connects the BBP transition~\cite{bbp_transition} \emph{quantitatively}
to Byzantine detection: we derive the exact threshold $\sigma^2\beta^2 = 0.25$ at which
MP-law identification becomes information-theoretically impossible, using the formal
Frequent Directions guarantee~\cite{ghashami_fd} and the minimax lower bound
of~\cite{byzantine_ml}.
Blockchain FL~\cite{blockchain_fl,blockchain_smart} has focused on auditability; we
formalize the ledger as a self-stabilizing shared memory substrate~\cite{garay_backbone}
with Byzantine fault tolerance strictly stronger than classical shared registers~\cite{awerbuch_varghese}.

\subsection{Positioning}

Our work differs along three axes.
\textbf{(i) Self-stabilization vs.\ robustness:}
Prior methods~\cite{krum,bulyan,crfl,flame,byzshield,karimireddy_bucketing} converge from
a \emph{known-good} initialization; none satisfy closure-and-convergence~\cite{arora_gouda}
from corrupted initial states.
We provide $T^* = O(\Delta_0(\sigma^2\beta^2/\varepsilon^2 + \beta^2/\varepsilon))$
under the practical self-stabilization model of~\cite{dolev_kat_practical}.
\textbf{(ii) Spectral vs.\ pairwise distance:}
Krum, Bulyan, and FLTrust operate on pairwise distances, which degrade at $\beta > 1/3$~\cite{farhadkhani_byzsgd}.
Our MP-law filter on the global eigenvalue distribution~\cite{bbp_transition} remains
effective to $\beta < 1/2$.
\textbf{(iii) Blockchain as stabilizing medium vs.\ auditability:}
Prior blockchain FL targets auditability; we use write-once BFT consensus~\cite{garay_backbone}
as the Byzantine-fault-tolerant analog of Dijkstra's shared registers,
enabling Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}~\cite{awerbuch_varghese,sss_blockchain_sharding}.
