\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented the first Byzantine-robust federated learning protocol with formal
practical self-stabilization guarantees~\cite{arora_gouda,dolev_kat_practical}.
Under Assumptions~1--5 and bounded initialization ($F(w^0)-F^*\leq\Delta_0$), the
protocol autonomously recovers to a legitimate configuration within
$T^* = O(\Delta_0(\sigma^2\beta^2/\varepsilon^2 + \beta^2/\varepsilon))$ rounds, achieving
$O(\sigma\beta/\sqrt{T} + \beta^2/T)$ convergence via a four-stage pipeline: Frequent
Directions sketching, Marchenko-Pastur spectral filtering, Byzantine-excluded aggregation,
and BFT on-chain checkpointing.
The BBP phase transition at $\sigma^2\beta^2 = 0.25$ yields a matching detection
impossibility~\cite{bbp_transition,byzantine_ml}, and empirical results across 12 attack
types show 78.4\% accuracy at 40\% Byzantine fraction---15 points above FLTrust~\cite{crfl}.

Open problems: fully decentralized spectral aggregation (no trusted coordinator),
asynchronous self-stabilization with bounded delay, tight Non-IID finite-sample
concentration bounds, DP integration preserving the phase-transition condition, and
blockchain overhead reduction at scale (Appendix~\ref{app:game_theory}).
